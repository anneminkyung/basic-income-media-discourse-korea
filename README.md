# Media Discourse on UBI-Related Cash Transfer Policies in South Korea

## Overview
This repository contains the code and analysis for a research project examining how South Korean media discourse has evolved around Universal Basic Income (UBI)–related and quasi-UBI cash transfer policies. The project analyzes media coverage over time to identify patterns of political contestation, sentiment dynamics, and the gradual normalization of policy experimentation.

The study focuses on how UBI-like policies are framed during electoral periods versus non-electoral periods, and how attention shifts from ideological debate to concrete, locally implemented policy initiatives.

## Research Questions
- How does media sentiment toward UBI-related cash transfer policies change over time?
- How do election periods affect the framing and volatility of UBI-related discourse?
- What thematic shifts emerge as UBI-like policies move from political proposals to policy experimentation?

## Policy Cases Analyzed
The analysis covers major UBI-related and quasi-UBI initiatives in South Korea, including:
- Farmers’ Basic Income (농민기본소득)
- Artists’ Basic Income (예술인 기본소득)
- Youth Basic Income (청년기본소득)
- Disaster Relief Funds (재난지원금)
- Livelihood Recovery Support Funds (민생회복지원금)
- Livelihood Recovery Coupons / Vouchers (민생회복쿠폰 / 민생쿠폰)
- Safety Income (안심소득)

These programs share key characteristics with UBI, such as unconditional or broadly targeted cash transfers and limited means-testing.

## Methodology
- **Data Collection:** News articles from South Korean media outlets
- **Text Analysis:**  
  - Sentiment analysis  
  - Topic modeling  
  - Temporal trend analysis
- **Analytical Focus:**  
  - Electoral vs. non-electoral periods  
  - National-level debate vs. local policy implementation  
  - Political framing vs. administrative evaluation

## Key Findings (Brief)

* Media sentiment toward UBI-related policies becomes more volatile during presidential election periods.
* Over time, coverage increasingly shifts from ideological debate to concrete policy implementation and evaluation.
* Local-level policy experiments play a growing role in normalizing UBI-related cash transfer discussions.

## Limitations

* Media coverage may not fully reflect public opinion.
* Topic modeling results depend on preprocessing and model assumptions.
* Policy outcomes are not evaluated directly; the focus is on discourse.
